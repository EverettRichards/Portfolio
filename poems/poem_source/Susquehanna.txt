08/12/2026

I pitched my green tent
and embarked on the trails
of Old Susquehanna,
her trickling current
lying just out of sight,
but well within earshot.

I whistled along with the birds,
and for a while, I couldn't tell
if I was mimicking them,
or if they were mimicking me;
and it didn't seem to matter.

One anonymous bird
partook in this tango
for about five minutes,
and as we called back and forth,
it occurred to me
that between me and the bird,
one of us had perfect pitch,
and it certainly wasn't me.

The forest was so strange,
I wondered if I was dead;
but the trees were too green
for it to be hell,
and the bugs made it clear
that I wasn't in heaven.

At night, I lit a fire,
but I grew discontent,
so I piled it high
with dry logs and attachments,
and watched the yellow embers
drift through the smoke
and sting the night sky.

The fire is an intimate thing
whose splendor can't be felt,
and whose heat can't be captured,
in any smeared photograph,
nor can the sweat
beading thick on my forehead
be so sweet as when staring
face-to-face with the flames.

Twenty years from now,
will these mid-Atlantic summers
still stick to my brain
the way the hot, dewy air
today sticks to my skin?

Or will the breezes of time
slowly wash and erode
my sand-castle dreams
until they're nothing but mounds,
smooth and symmetrical,
just beyond the tide's reach?
