WIP
9/06/2026

I find myself on the shores of Atlantic City
and I realize that after all the times I've been to the east coast as a kid,
I don't know if I've ever touched the waters of the Atlantic Ocean.
And so I do.
Despite the grey color that I'm sure would repulse me by daylight,
and despite the foul odor that I'd inhale if my sinuses were clearer,
I still reach out and touch the water. Even though I know I shouldn't.
And it wasn't the moment of epiphany that I had anticipated.

And then I thought of Bruce Springsteen,
and how he sang a sad tale of Atlantic City,
and I tried to recall the lyrics,
as if I might find insights into another man's perspective
into this strange mirage of the anthropocene.

Springsteen begins,
"They blew up a chicken man in Philly last night
and now they blew up his house, too"
And every time I heard those words, I thought,
"What does that mean? That's absurd. That doesn't make any sense."
And now, here in Atlantic City, I've thought about it.

I still don't know what it means,
but I know how it *feels*.
I *get it*.

Looking out at that city, I *get it*.
I know the feeling that Bruce Springsteen had
when he, too,
decades ago,
surely stepped foot on the same beach
that my shallow footprints now inhabit
momentarily, until the next high tide.

And I see the casinos rising sharply out of the shore,
disrupting the otherwise-perfect landscape
and the boardwalk dancing gently along the seastrand
in the dark jungle-night,

And it occurs to me that Bruce Springsteen
never mentioned the beach, or the casinos, or the boardwalk,
but focused instead on deeper, more internal problems:
crime, poverty, and hopelessness.
These were the things he saw in this sinful city,
and these are the things I know I am full of.

But, here I am.
At the same place that inspired Bruce Springsteen
to have these obscure, absurd, melancholic visions
and words in his mind.

They blew up a chicken man in Philly last night.
