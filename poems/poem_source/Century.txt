* My one-hundredth poem. A conglomeration of all my poems, in order.

Awake are the pieces of nature's great puzzle,
didactic and yearning to break off its muzzle

I sit upon the fallen trunk of a fearsome oak;
I surrender to the current of the narrow brook.

The sky offers a halfhearted apology
to the drowning flowers of the valley.

A forgotten highway connects two distant towns,
but nobody is certain which town lies on the other side.

The sun and moon battle overhead for control of the sky,

but under six feet of earth, all is silent.

Sand rains down from the beach in the sky,

and the forest's gentle melodies creep through the air.

The sun knows it will set, the day knows it will end;

is there nothing about it that begs your allure?

The stars only dare to shine when there is nothing left to be seen,

but they evade my every attempt to grasp them
except in the shallow waters before each night's rest,
surfacing only when they are certain to be forgotten.

The streets are decked with starstruck folks
seeking escape from the air that chokes

for death is elusive,
and there is no eternity.

My trajectory will be uncertain for many years hence;

how many hours jam between none and one?
Time slowly chases, but you needn't run.

I look at the stars,
the stars do the same.

I bide my time until tomorrow's sun unzips an eager sky.

And try as I might to write senseful scrawl,
the words on my paper mean nothing at all.

My aching hands fall cold and sharp on worn concrete,

so beseech I the sandstone,
"Where am I going?"

Why do these words pierce my heart? /
Already fate has been set;
its maker, unmade, forever.

I cannot ignore
that I'm still the same person.

To know was once to wonder. /
Ask not about the soul
whose finance so endeavors /
Steel alone persists
from time to lonely time,

giving to the world
and the ocean from whence they were born,
with wherefore to give,

and to my surprise,
the sun's embers rise,

breaking softly
against a sky of fluffy white,

above the cascades
that thundered below,

a final response
to questions once asked
but now surely answered

as distance traded volume for tranquility.

Don't make plans for the future
before they're for sure.

So listen, dear stranger:

I am aware that I am lost.

With eyes glassy and naive,

burning like foxfire on a misty green knoll,

we traipse with haste and valor

into pitch-black horizons,

and always will we succumb
to the halting hand of time;

And as I stare
at the silver strands
of cottonball whiskey,

we are friends nonetheless.

Like houses soiled down,

the wind carries promise
to beckon an era

honey-ripe with duress,

and I reminded myself
of warm years hence,

when autumn moons
sang out their tunes

to let down-fall
my castle wall

in a strange way,
like it never had before / 
before crossing the threshold
of the knowing and the known.

Many moments have since passed me by;

still, there is power
in their sinister silence.

For a while, I stared
at the fuzzy black sky
and wondered,

what novelty do the stars afford me?

I listened intently,
but I couldn't make out

the lines of a soft, satin song,

melting away, abruptly
into the bible-black dawn,

ripe with adolescent rain.

Bright streamers
streak across the sky
in brilliant discord,

bitingly bitter,

and the sidewalks teem with ice.

I have an opportunity
to break this wayward spell,

and I think for a moment
of the places I've been,

of yester-noon 'ventures
'tween holiday nights;

familiar scenes
flashed through my mind

and in spite of my insistence
against fate and free will,

we needn't concede
the strange lives that we please.

Will the breezes of time
slowly wash and erode

this lush Ohio summer?

It's another long day
turning thoughts into worries

and at first, this unsettles me.

I see craters and canyons
that tell me I'll never come home.

But havoc does unfold

and I notice at once
that I am an intruder,

but please see
the compassionate strife

and the whitewater currents
that thrash through tough rocks.

It stings to know I've walked astray
to chase beautiful lies,

and I hoped that they'd someday
be true once again,

and I can't help
but ask myself,

can you ever be ready
for something so unknown?

And now, as my soles slowly sink
into this sandy Sunday evening,

I just can't seem
to escape the clear fact
that I'm here where I am,

my feelings all flustered
and my claims all refuted.

I blink and rub my eyes.

My black and silver bicycle
has dirt between its treads.

It might never have seen
the sunlight again,

like burden-free whispers
in their ramshackle homes.

I've looked for love
in all the same old places

where I make my night's vice,

where on all but the mistiest days,
the everclear sky would shine

with the quick-changing wind
a brisk harbinger of winter,

though I don't really care
to stick around here

so much as to be
endearingly understood.

Heaven and hell
are one and the same,

chasing bone-breaking pavement
over blood pedigree;

in this forested place,
I am born again.

Another season has passed.

The golden spring sun no longer hastens to rise.
