#stormy
7/28/26

I climb aboard my silver-steel wagon
and click the engine into roaring life.
The metallic choking subsides to a purr,
and the tiny digital display blinks alight.
On the dashboard, a warning flickers on,
bordered in hazard-orange:
"Tire Pressure Sensor Fault."
I press "OK" to dismiss, as I always do,
and proceed with no due caution,
because a broken sensor does no wrong
(unless the thing it's monitoring does).

And I place my phone above the cupholders
that seldom carry any weight,
tucked beneath the lip of the center console
whose hinge has recently been mended,
and beside the vacant silhouette
of what could once retain a cell phone,
but now could not fit widthwise
what it was meant to fit lengthwise.

And I grab the crooked handle
that sticks out from behind the steering wheel
and pull it towards me,
and then down a notch,
into reverse,
and then I release it;
and I gently maneuver
this two-ton beast
between crushed green plants
and a pristine Jeep;
and then I shift two more notches down,
reset the wheel,
and climb that asphalt ramp
that bridges my haven-like driveway
to the unpainted street.

And I cruise down High Valley,
its curves thrashing 'round
'tween the mounds and cliffs
that I've so often navigated,
and barely scrape by
the tameless bushes
that eerily creep
their fingers over the road,
occasionally brushing
against my hard skin.

And I hastily drive
to wherever I'm headed,
carelessly heeding
the rules of the road,
and I think for a moment
of the places I've been,
and the miles I've buried
beneath mountains of time,
and I remind myself at once
of the bright face that awaits me
just seven miles away.
