9/27/26
* The first four stanzas were written on a road trip from San Diego to Denver in January of 2025, along with my girlfriend at the time. The rest was written in September of 2026.

O' splendid, sunny Phoenix,
your pale green eyebrow
atop a sandy mountainscape,
what tidings do you bear?
Only your humble neighbor, Tucson,
shares your gentle desert air;

Next we go to New Mexico,
where the sun hangs low
above the barren arroyo
and beneath the clear blue sky,
a desert asleep under sun and moon,

her white sands enveloped
by the cloak of the night
except for the occasional
shimmering city lights
like beacons to a distant world,
begging to be witnessed.

We exchange in Las Cruces
and to West Texas we go,
to the storied old town of El Paso,
where tumbleweeds grow
and the Rio Grande flows,
a gentle reminder
that heaven and hell
are one and the same,
divided only by imagination
and by God's cruel gambit;

---

and then we head north
as the ashy snow falls,
and we find ourselves sliding
through Colorado Springs,
my mirrors and windows
all opaque and iced up,
and with gas costs so low,
we can't help but fill up.

Trace the Rockies
and drive along I-25,
and up next, you'll find
God's glorious Denver,
asleep as the sun waits
to eagerly rise
and bring violet and orange
to brisk mile-high skies.

But Durango, my Lord,
there is no English word
to describe the deep longing
I have since felt for you;
in a bookstore by the river,
my love felt renewed,
and I know no place better
to relax for some time
in between the vast highways
that will soon take me home.

And, oh, those Four Corners
that I've dreamed of since birth,
so gorgeous and textured
with the pains of the Earth,
and I see very clearly
the canyons and pillars
that keep out the land-man
and leave this place sacred.

But Laughlin, I see
plain right through your deceit!
Though you shimmer so tall
in the crest of Nevada,
I won't let you fool me
to think you're Las Vegas;
though your brandished casinos
sure tempt me to stay.

And, of course, the Grand Canyon --
I'd never forget to tell!
Though I've seen you before,
my memory could never mimic
the grandeur you instill
in my frost-barren heart;
your rocky veins spread wide
like the life that you nourish,
and though it's fifteen degrees there,
I'm warm here in my car.

Even San Bernardino,
though she's often forgotten
or said to have nothing to offer,
holds beauty, still
in her beige desert flora;
and I now truly know,
as the minutes pass by,
that America's largest county
is not just a fun fact,
but a harsh, dry reality.

And then, finally, I'm home
in my sweet San Diego,
her careless palm trees
swaying upright and firm,
and I feel a warm breeze
for the first time in weeks
as the sea-faring wind
gently kisses my cheek;

and I know I am glad
to have seen the great land
and America's best,
in my desert southwest.
