9/23/26

For as long as I can remember,
blue was my favorite color.

I first became acquainted with the sky in San Diego,
where on all but the mistiest days,
the everclear sky would shine

bright and true-blue 'tween the clouds,
on the rare occasion they colluded
to season her skin,

but most days, so plain
as a sapphire capsule
above the copper chaparral.

The sky, to me,
was the happier cousin
of the boat-faring sea,

who'd thrash 'round with angst
when the rains battered down
their sour contempt,

condemned forever
to bring life to her depths
quite more often than death,

yet still charging tolls
to the homeward-bound sailors
who brave her warning waves.

How strange it was, then,
that these two great machines
share dolor and color,
flavor and hue,

and that life once did spring
from the volcanic depths
in a phosphorus haze,

and that life now ascends
to the heavenly skies
upon wings of feather or aluminum;

and yet, down on Earth
you will seldom here find
a lone azure companion
or a royal facade

'cept the false dyes and pigments
that Man hath procured
with Promethean science,

a gentle reminder
that God, or Nature,
or whoever's in charge,

meant first to reserve blue
for the boiling sea
and the startling sky,

and that Man's great protest
was to capture her hue
and to brand it to denim
and Elmer's School Glue.

Blue, my dear hue,
never stop being you.
