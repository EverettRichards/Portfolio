* This poem was spawned by a realization I had while sitting on the sofa and drinking spiced cinnamon apple tea: I was not born to be productive. I was born to experience and enjoy and explore this world, and all its faults.

...

I find myself on the shore
of a still-water lagoon
and I perch upon a boulder
to stare at my reflection,
engulfed in shallow ripples.

My eyes trace the lines
that have slowly encroached
on my red marble face
and I am reminded of fault lines,
San Andreas and the like,

which weave through the land,
sometimes invisibly,
like the calloused bronze hands
of a time-seasoned farmer
who works the fields by night;

and yet waiting, with ire,
to swiftly strike and envenomate
the fleshy heel of man,
like a coiled black mamba
who lurks in the reeds.

And I see, once again,
though past times I've forgotten,
that the toils and vipers
of this enigmatic world
should not spawn in you despair,

but a gentle reminder
that we have no purposes
but those which we humbly
ascribe to ourselves
in moments surreal,

and that the heavy-weight wheels
that command our routines
are indifferent to us,
and we needn't concede
the strange lives that we please,

lest we fail to decree,
when the light finally dies,
that our lives were well worth it,
that our shared pain was beautiful,
and that we tried.
