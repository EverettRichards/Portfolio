* An extended metaphor comparing the presidency of James K. Polk (1845-1849) to my love life, including the Mexican-American War and the dispute with the British in Oregon Country.
* The title, "Fifty-Four Forty or Fight", was a campaign slogan of Polk's in 1844, advocating for the US to take complete control of Oregon Country (and if they don't give it to us, we will go to war for it!!!), which was then jointly controlled by the US and Britain, and which had a northern border at 54°40' latitude.
* But, eventually, Polk realized that it would be too difficult to take control of Oregon Country, so he settled for the lower half, which is now the US states of Washington, Oregon, and Idaho, while letting Britain keep the upper half, now the Canadian province of British Columbia.
* Sometimes, you have to accept that something is not worth fighting for, and settle for a split that will make both sides happier in the long run...

* An excerpt from Chat GPT's review: "You're essentially coping with romantic loss by demanding the entire Pacific Northwest from Britain. Very normal behavior. 😂"


Consider me Polk
with a musket and mullet,
austere, yet provoked
by a Mexican bullet;

and call our fate Thornton,
our manner disputed,
our feelings all flustered
and our claims all refuted;

and reckon with tales
of an imbalanced war,
two-years, yet one-sided
and now, nevermore;

and unfurl your flag
with a bear and dull star,
and revolt like Sonoma
'fore I head to the bar.

So, give to me Oregon,
with its hand-crafted beers
and her volcanic lakes
fed by sky-fallen tears;

and give to me Idaho,
where old friends reside,
and panhandlers panhandle
on roads far and wide;

and give to me Washington,
her forests so deep,
where brisk rivers trickle
and mountaintops sleep;

and give to me Vancouver,
old island of rain,
though indeed, I might trade it
if it yields too much pain;

and give to me ev'rything
upon this bleak land,
for it ne'er will compare
to the warmth of your hand,

but just know, though I write you,
no more love do I affirm,
for I plainly must tell you
I'll seek no second term.
