* A short poem about how time seems to keep moving faster and faster and faster...
* I was discussing this idea with a friend when the words came to me: Every time I blink, it's Thursday night.
* After jotting the original (now final) stanza down, I was reminded of the line from John Denver's song "Poems, Prayers, and Promises": "The days they pass so quickly now, nights are seldom long"
* ^ and the Barenaked Ladies in "Lovers in a Dangerous Time": "Don't the hours grow shorter as the days go by?"

* Anyways, here's the poem...

See how the hours wane as days go by,
and count the roaming deadlines as they fall,
and notice that there's stillness in the sky,
above the glens that jetty up so tall.

And if you feel inclined to trace the bed
where ivy weaves its way 'long walls of brick,
be humble as you heave your heavy head,
or let the next day greet you with a crick.

Am I to blame for autumn's flaming leaves?
I really can't imagine being so;
yet still, they fall and gather on my eaves
while on my porch, I catch the dew below.

Despite the way the sun breaks through the haze,
and though I'd say that things are quite alright,
time's jaunty jaws just jumble up my days,
and every time I blink, it's Thursday night.
