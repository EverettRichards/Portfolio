#stormy
8/22/26

* I wrote the first draft of this poem while kayaking at Lums Pond in Delaware on a cloudy late-August Saturday morning.
This poem means several things. Below is a summary...
1. In many ways, it is a literal transcription of the thoughts and ideas I had while looking at the green trees around me and grey sky above me.
2. It is a metaphor for humanity's tendency to impose itself upon nature, but also a recognition that we contribute to the same nature we destroy by recognizing its beauty.
3. It is also a metaphor for moving across the country and seeing the green landscape of the east coast, the grey eyes I see everywhere (you know who you are), and the San Diego beach sunset memories I bring with me (and that keep me afloat!)

I come into the presence
of near-still water
whose crestless waves
gently lap my kayak
and I look above,
to the place I imagine
that the stars would be
if it were night
and the sky was clear
of those impenetrable grey clouds
that steal the blue from the sky
and from the pond's mirror surface
mere inches below me.

Meanwhile, great trees
seem to rise like dust
out of the water,
but I know they conceal the shore
between their roots
and their chlorophyll masks,
which stampede and contest each other
for a chance to be graced by sunlight.

I see twigs and logs
drifting sparsely
through the silent water,
and I know they once sprang
from those sturdy bronze limbs
that stretch over the water,
like a botanic wall
delivering dainty, damp driftwood
while separating my oars
from the world beyond the pond.

And I notice at once
that I am an intruder,
with my flame-graded vessel
and caution-yellow oars,
and even my blue life jacket
taints the pure view
of variant, vibrant, verdant greens,
so lush and so saturated with life,
and the soft grey sky,
a canvas for the day.

And I see that the water's palette
is a murky mix
of these two spectra
with green, my new life,
and grey, like stormy eyes,
and here I am,
bringing blue, red, and orange,
the colors of the ever-evolving sky
that harkens the fields
and forests of the east,
and of the pale Moonlight sunsets
I squinted at, with wonder,
so many years ago,
to a landscape
so humbly and momentarily deprived
of such a privileged light.

Who am I to tell Lums Pond
what colors should skim its surface?

For the hues that I'm used to
do not belong
where they are not naturally found,
but when the sun and the sky
agree to make it so.

For now, I'll look outward
to the bichromatic panorama
that stretches out before me,
and take in its great splendor,
all the while acknowledging
that I am a poison to this land,
but a poison that notices
and observes, and assigns beauty,
and so I know that I am right
to do what I do
and to be here, and to exist,
and to bid thanks to the landscape
that I,
and my wiley compatriots,
live amidst.
