* I wrote this poem during and after a spontaneous four-day road trip in my brand new Subaru Outback, which I slept in the back of.
I drove from Delaware through Maryland, West Virginia, Kentucky, Indiana, Ohio, and Pennsylvania.
I was amazed by the stark green-ness of the landscape, and how every state I visited was just as vibrant as the last.

...

The pre-processed grief
and rural fantasies
you selfishly concoct
will not change the fact

that the fleeting sunset
you burn your eyes for
would never do
the same for you.

While the day star shines,
I’m guided by glints
shining sharply and white
off of foreign-made cars;

how many sleepless nights
have I spent by your door,
in a dimly-lit lot
beneath Subaru stars?

Now the green, I see,
has turned commodity,
and it fills in the canvas
with a luxury hue,

not one I’m used to,
but one that enthralled me
when I first saw her,
a gem among lemons,

and I knew then,
like I ne’er before did,
just what it was
that I’d wanted and been missing.

For, at home, the rural
was uncompromising desert,
and the suburbs a far cry
from indigenous landscapes,

and yet, in this lush Ohio summer,
the untamed grass spreads free and ripe,
embracing the water
from the river and from the sky.

I wonder what lies ahead,
and what sits beyond
my sights of prospection
and my “known unknowns”,

and I know I’m too strong
to be ‘suaded by God,
but too weak to accept
that the Earth has no steward

but the negligent hand
of the steel-smelting man,
who shows ore to the sun
and extracts its blinding radiance.

I see, then, that though
the years threaten
to strip me of my
bright-eyed wonder,

they also offer
to beckon a new light
to pave in my shadowed
valleys of perspective,

and to stoke the cool flames
of awe-struck inquisition
more profoundly than any
individual answer,

and to give to me a purpose,
an intrinsic merit,
and something so grand
for my kin to inherit.
