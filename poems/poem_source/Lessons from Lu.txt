9/11/26

Lu,
I never knew myself
until I knew you.

I rattled and bumped
through my long teenage days
like a splintered sheet of plywood
hanging out of an old pickup truck,

not too different from the Ford
we used to coyly retreat to
when the harsh high-school halls
protested our young love.

I felt angry, and sad,
like all young folks do,
and I lacked the tools needed
to manage that load,

but then you came along
and you caressed my shaking hand
and you told me to process;
to lend thoughts to my feelings.

And at first, I rejected
your gentle advance,
afraid of the regions
of my mind left unexplored,

but I saw in your eyes--
your deep, dark brown eyes--
that you knew how it felt
to feel trapped and alone.

And I know now, indeed,
I could never repay you
for the lessons you gave me
at such an opportune time,

and that I'd never be caught dead
writing poems, like these,
if it weren't for the safety
and peace I saw in you.

And I know you were right,
though I didn't always see it,
for my eyes were too focused
on external gazes,

and though I can't quite recall
the sound of your laugh,
I'm still a better man
having known you in the past.

I'll admit that I haven't much
sent thoughts your way,
for these three years apart
have been so busy and strange,

but this week, I just can't seem
to escape the clear fact
that I'm here where I am
partly because of you,

and because of the way
you inspired me, dearly
to rush towards my dreams
and be kind to myself.

Lu,
I never knew myself
until I knew you.
