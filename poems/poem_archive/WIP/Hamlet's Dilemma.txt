WIP

8/18/2026

In my life, I have known too many people who have contemplated suicide.
Friends, family and strangers alike.
When asked about their reasons (or when that information is volunteered),
I always hear the same thing.

Life is meaningless.
I have no purpose.
There's no point to existing.
The world is cruel, unjust, and painful.

But when I ask people what they think the meaning of life is,
no two answers are alike.

To serve your community.
To serve God.
To prepare the next generation to live in a peaceful world.
To leave the world better than you found it.
To laugh.
To cry.
To love.
To enjoy all the world's offerings, brilliant and mundane alike.
To make people happy.
To find happiness for yourself.
To teach.
To learn.
To win.
To earn.
To be productive.
To have fun.
To kick back and drink a cold one.
To celebrate.
To mourn.
To praise and to scorn.
To uplift and uphold,
To deny and depose.
To exist.

And at first, this unsettles me.
In many matters I am familiar with,
there is one single right answer and many wrong ones.
What is the capital of Indonesia?
There is only one Jakarta.
And so, I feel saddened
to think that maybe,
death and nonexistence *are* the solution to the great question and mystery of life,
and everything else is just an illusion
in a desperate attempt to justify our continued existence.

But then I realize
that true, transcendent meaning
is not bound by the arbitrary correlations we find in our man-made logic,
but rather, it is free to be whatever one wishes
and that all the reasons to exist are valid reasons
as long as they encourage you to keep existing and searching for fulfillment in your own way.

The cup is not half empty, nor is it half full. You're mincing words. The cup merely is. The cup and the water merely coexist.
